Unit 1: Foundations of Thinking & Mind-Brain-Behavior Link

Thinking: A higher-order cognitive process involving the internal manipulation, transformation, and representation of mental structures (concepts, symbols, images, and rules) to solve problems, reason, make decisions, and construct novel knowledge.

The Mind / Brain / Behavior Interplay

  • The Brain (Biological Substrate / Hardware): Physical neurological structure comprising roughly 86 billion neurons, neural networks, synapses, neurotransmitters, and specialized regions (e.g., prefrontal cortex for executive control, amygdala for emotional processing, hippocampus for memory).
  • The Mind (Cognitive Domain / Software): The information-processing faculty comprising conscious and unconscious processes, including perception, mental imagery, beliefs, logical flow, desires, and subjective experience.
  • Behavior (Physical Manifestation / Output): Observable external actions, motor executions, verbal communication, and decisions triggered by the mind's cognitive processing of neural inputs.
  • Dynamic Loop: Stimuli $\rightarrow$ Brain activity $\rightarrow$ Mind processes meaning and triggers emotion $\rightarrow$ Observable Behavior $\rightarrow$ Environmental feedback rewires neural connections via neuroplasticity.

Unit 2: Analytical Thinking vs. Critical Thinking

Dimension Analytical Thinking Critical Thinking
Core Nature Deconstructive, linear, step-by-step. Evaluative, skeptical, holistic, objective.
Primary Goal Break complex systems down into constituent elements to identify causes, mechanics, and patterns. Judge validity, logical consistency, soundness, underlying assumptions, and biases in assertions.
Guiding Question "How does this break down? What are the underlying pieces?" "Is this premise logically sound? What biases exist?"
Technical Context Code profiling, algorithmic complexity analysis ($O(n)$), hardware failure analysis. Evaluating cloud architectural trade-offs, security threat modeling, algorithmic fairness auditing.

Unit 3: Critical Thinking and Learning

1. Reasoning Paradigms & Predictions

  • Deductive Reasoning (Top-Down): Moves from broad axiomatic truths to logically certain specific conclusions. (If premises are true, conclusion must be true).
  • Inductive Reasoning (Bottom-Up): Synthesizes specific, repeated empirical observations into generalized probabilities (basis of machine learning models).
  • Abductive Reasoning: Infers the simplest and most plausible hypothesis from incomplete facts (used in root-cause fault debugging).
  • Making Predictions: Developing predictive internal mental models to project future outcomes, anticipate system vulnerabilities, and plan edge cases.

2. Memory & Critical Thinking

  • Working Memory: Transient cognitive workspace (capacity: $7 \pm 2$ chunks). Limits the volume of dynamic variables a thinker can evaluate concurrently.
  • Long-Term Memory: Houses schemas, stored domain knowledge, and mental representations that anchor rapid contextual reasoning.
  • Cognitive Biases: Reconstructive recall is vulnerable to confirmation bias and misinformation effects, requiring structured checklists to prevent biased judgment.

3. Emotions & Critical Thinking

  • Cognitive Interference: High affective arousal (panic, stress) leads to amygdala-mediated hijacking, impairing executive function in the prefrontal cortex.
  • Cognitive Appraisal Theory: Emotions are generated by how an individual cognitively frames an event; self-regulation prevents emotional skewing of facts.
  • Emotional Intelligence as an Asset: Empathy and emotional awareness provide crucial moral, interpersonal, and ethical guardrails for engineering decisions.

4. Thinking Skills (Bloom's Taxonomy)

  • Lower-Order Thinking Skills (LOTS): Remembering (recalling data) $\rightarrow$ Understanding (explaining meaning) $\rightarrow$ Applying (using procedures in routine tasks).
  • Higher-Order Thinking Skills (HOTS): Analyzing (deconstructing systems) $\rightarrow$ Evaluating (critiquing and judging by criteria) $\rightarrow$ Creating (synthesizing elements into novel systems).

Unit 4: The Eureka Moment (Insight Problem Solving)

The Eureka Moment (the Aha! experience) is the sudden, conscious realization of an optimal solution to an intractable problem following a prolonged mental impasse.

Problem Presentation ──> Mental Impasse ──> Incubation ──> Cognitive Restructuring ──> Eureka / Illumination
  1. Mental Impasse: Direct analytical approaches stall due to mental fixation or functional fixedness (viewing components solely in conventional ways).
  2. Incubation Phase: Conscious work ceases; attention is shifted elsewhere. The unconscious mind maintains low-level, parallel associative processing across disparate semantic networks.
  3. Cognitive Restructuring: Unconscious processing discards erroneous self-imposed constraints and reformulates the mental model of the problem space.
  4. Illumination (Eureka): The restructured pathways reach consciousness in a sudden flash of clarity, accompanied by subjective certainty and positive affect.

Unit 5: Creativity & Creative Thinking

Definition: Creativity is the cognitive capacity to produce outcomes or ideas that are simultaneously novel (original, unexpected) and appropriate (practical, useful, and compliant with real-world constraints).

Convergent vs. Divergent Thinking (J.P. Guilford)

Divergent Thinking (Idea Generation) Convergent Thinking (Idea Evaluation)
Explores outward in multiple directions from a single prompt. Narrows inward from multiple possibilities toward one optimal solution.
Measured by:
  • Fluency: Raw volume of generated ideas.
  • Flexibility: Diversity of categories/domains used.
  • Originality: Novelty and statistical rarity of ideas.
  • Elaboration: Level of nuance and refinement added.
 Applies logical rigor, standard evaluation rubrics, constraints, and trade-off matrices to arrive at decisions.

Brainstorming: Idea Generation & Evaluation (Alex Osborn)

  • Phase 1: Divergent Generation Rules:
    1. Defer Judgment: Strictly separate ideation from evaluation (criticism halts creative momentum).
    2. Encourage Wild Ideas: Unconventional ideas expand the solution space and break mental sets.
    3. Go for Quantity: High volume maximizes the likelihood of uncovering breakthrough concepts.
    4. Combine and Improve (Piggybacking): Build, iterate, and hybridize existing ideas.
  • Phase 2: Convergent Evaluation: Screen ideas using structured filters such as PMI (Plus, Minus, Interesting) and Feasibility vs. Impact Matrices.

Image Generation, Evaluation & Debating

  • Image Generation: The cognitive capacity to project, manipulate, and simulate visual-spatial systems internally (mental wireframes, architectures) without sensory input.
  • Image Evaluation: Mentally inspecting and simulating operational stress on dynamic representations to verify edge-case viability before execution.
  • Debating: A structured dialectic exercise that strengthens creative and critical thinking by challenging cognitive biases, testing premises against counterarguments, and rooting out logical fallacies in real time.

Unit 6: Geoff Petty’s ICEDIP Model of Creative Thinking

Geoff Petty defines creativity as non-linear work requiring shifts between six distinct mental attitudes or "phases."

Phase Adopted Mindset Core Actions & Objectives
I - Inspiration Playful, Adventurous, Non-judgmental Generate broad pools of raw ideas; engage in lateral thinking and free association; avoid premature filtering.
C - Clarification Focused, Strategic, Clear Unpack the true underlying objective, define boundary constraints, success metrics, and client/user requirements.
E - Evaluation Skeptical, Discerning, Analytical Critique ideas objectively; identify structural vulnerabilities, functional failure points, and edge cases.
D - Distillation Selective, Pragmatic, Decisive Select the most viable concepts from the Inspiration pool; prioritize ideas and shape them into actionable plans.
I - Incubation Detached, Relaxed, Patient Intentionally step away from the work; allow subconscious associative processing to resolve mental blocks.
P - Perspiration Methodical, Disciplined, Tenacious Put in the systematic effort required to implement, build, refine, and deliver a fully functional end product.

High-Yield Exam Quick Recap

  • Mind / Brain / Behavior: Brain = Biological Hardware $\rightarrow$ Mind = Cognitive Software $\rightarrow$ Behavior = Physical Executed Action.
  • Analytical vs. Critical: Analytical = Deconstructive/Mechanistic; Critical = Evaluative/Assessing validity & logic.
  • Eureka Cycle: Problem Encounter $\rightarrow$ Impasse $\rightarrow$ Incubation (Unconscious) $\rightarrow$ Cognitive Restructuring $\rightarrow$ Illumination (Aha!).
  • Core Criterion of Creativity: Must possess both Novelty and Appropriateness/Utility.
  • Guilford's Divergent Metrics: Fluency (count), Flexibility (categories), Originality (rarity), Elaboration (detail).
  • Petty's ICEDIP Framework: Inspiration, Clarification, Evaluation, Distillation, Incubation, Perspiration.
